Chapter 19 - UNTIL THE DAY YOU DIE

My auntie had 8 weeks to live.

She was in hospital with another stroke. The cancer was destroying her insides.

Auntie had just made it out and lived out her final 4 weeks at home. 3 weeks before she died, I visited and took her through a small stroke recovery session.

“Has any therapist visited her to help her with her stroke recovery” I asked the immediate family.

No.

She was stage 4 and weeks or days away from death. What was the point? We did a session anyway.

I helped relieve some muscular tension, helped her move her legs a little and helped her to sit upright. It was the tiniest workout I ever took anyone through.

It wouldn't have made much of a difference, if at all. But she was willing.

And she gave it everything she had, to a 7/10 effort. Two seated calf raises, two supported arm lifts and three deep breathes together.

She was in so much agony.

I wish I had done more.

It’s never too late to do something, and at the end it might not even seem like it matters. But it could matter right now.

The possibility of connection with our body is always there. An arm movement, a spinal roll, a half hearted karate back kick into a tennis serve in the middle of the lounge.

It’s all movement. Its discovery, It's keeping blood flow at least. It might be too late to compete in the Olympics, but it’s never too late to do something.
